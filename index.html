The Ladder of Time

Ascending from the blink of an eye to the dissolution of Brahmā, as given in the Manusmṛti and the Purāṇas.

Within one day (Manusmṛti 1.64)
  • 1 nimeṣa — a twinkling of the eye ≈ 0.1778 s (8/45 s)
  • 18 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā — ≈ 3.2 seconds
  • 30 kāṣṭhā = 1 kalā — 540 nimeṣa ≈ 96 seconds
  • 30 kalā = 1 muhūrta — 16,200 nimeṣa ≈ 48 minutes
  • 30 muhūrta = 1 ahorātra — 4,86,000 nimeṣa = 24 hours
Calendar
  • 15 ahorātra = 1 pakṣa — a fortnight
  • 2 pakṣa = 1 māsa — a month (the day-and-night of the Pitṛs)
  • 2 māsa = 1 ṛtu — a season
  • 3 ṛtu = 1 ayana — six months (sun's northward or southward course)
  • 2 ayana = 1 varṣa — a human year = 1 divine day & night
Cosmic scale (in divine years of the gods)
  • Kali Yuga — 1,200 divine years = 4,32,000 human years
  • Dvāpara Yuga — 2,400 divine years = 8,64,000 human years
  • Tretā Yuga — 3,600 divine years = 12,96,000 human years
  • Satya (Kṛta) Yuga — 4,800 divine years = 17,28,000 human years
  • 1 Mahāyuga (Caturyuga) — 12,000 divine years = 43,20,000 human years
  • 71 Mahāyuga = 1 Manvantara — the reign of one Manu ≈ 30.67 crore human years
  • 14 Manvantaras = 1 Kalpa — also exactly 1,000 Mahāyugas = 1 day of Brahmā = 432 crore human years
  • 1 day + 1 night of Brahmā — 2 Kalpas = 864 crore human years
  • 360 days & nights = 1 year of Brahmā — 3.1104 trillion human years
  • 100 years of Brahmā = 1 Mahākalpa — 311.04 trillion human years, ending in Mahāpralaya

What the Scriptures Say

The same ladder appears across the texts — but the smallest ratios differ from book to book. Open each to read the verses.

Manusmṛti (Chapter 1) 18 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā

1.64 — The base ladder. Eighteen nimeṣa make one kāṣṭhā; thirty kāṣṭhā, one kalā; thirty kalā, one muhūrta; and thirty muhūrta, one ahorātra.

निमेषा दश चाष्टौ च काष्ठा त्रिंशत् तु ताः कला । त्रिंशत् कला मुहूर्तः स्यादहोरात्रं तु तावतः ॥ ६४ ॥
nimeṣā daśa cāṣṭau ca kāṣṭhā triṃśat tu tāḥ kalā | triṃśat kalā muhūrtaḥ syād ahorātraṃ tu tāvataḥ || 64 ||
“Ten and eight nimeṣas are one kāṣṭhā; thirty kāṣṭhās one kalā; thirty kalās one muhūrta; and as many muhūrtas, one ahorātra (day and night).”
— Manusmṛti 1.64

1.65–1.67 — Human, ancestral and divine days. The sun divides day and night; a month is the day-and-night of the Pitṛs (manes); and a year is one day-and-night of the gods — Uttarāyaṇa their day, Dakṣiṇāyana their night.

अहोरात्रे विभजते सूर्यो मानुषदैविके । रात्रिः स्वप्नाय भूतानां चेष्टायै कर्मणामहः ॥ ६५ ॥
ahorātre vibhajate sūryo mānuṣadaivike | rātriḥ svapnāya bhūtānāṃ ceṣṭāyai karmaṇām ahaḥ || 65 ||
“The sun divides the days and nights of humans and of the gods: night for the sleep of beings, day for the performance of works.”
— Manusmṛti 1.65
दैवे रात्र्यहनी वर्षं प्रविभागस्तयोः पुनः । अहस्तत्रोदगयनं रात्रिः स्याद् दक्षिणायनम् ॥ ६७ ॥
daive rātryahanī varṣaṃ pravibhāgas tayoḥ punaḥ | ahas tatrodagayanaṃ rātriḥ syād dakṣiṇāyanam || 67 ||
“A year is a day and a night of the gods: the northward course of the sun is their day, the southward course their night.”
— Manusmṛti 1.67

1.69–1.73 — The Yugas and the day of Brahmā. Kṛta is 4,000 divine years (with twilight hundreds); the others fall by a quarter; the total is 12,000 divine years = one Mahāyuga; and 1,000 such Mahāyugas are one day of Brahmā, with a night of equal length.

यदेतत् परिसङ्ख्यातमादावेव चतुर्युगम् । एतद् द्वादशसाहस्रं देवानां युगमुच्यते ॥ ७१ ॥
yad etat parisaṅkhyātam ādāv eva caturyugam | etad dvādaśasāhasraṃ devānāṃ yugam ucyate || 71 ||
“These four ages, thus reckoned, are together called one age of the gods — twelve thousand (divine years).”
— Manusmṛti 1.71
दैविकानां युगानां तु सहस्रं परिसङ्ख्यया । ब्राह्ममेकमहर्ज्ञेयं तावतीं रात्रिमेव च ॥ ७२ ॥
daivikānāṃ yugānāṃ tu sahasraṃ parisaṅkhyayā | brāhmam ekam ahar jñeyaṃ tāvatīṃ rātrim eva ca || 72 ||
“A thousand ages of the gods is to be known as one day of Brahmā, and his night is of the same length.”
— Manusmṛti 1.72

1.79 — Manvantara. Twelve thousand divine years, multiplied by seventy-one, is here called a Manvantara (the period of one Manu).

यद् प्राग् द्वादशसाहस्रमुदितं दैविकं युगम् । तदेकसप्ततिगुणं मन्वन्तरमिहोच्यते ॥ ७९ ॥
yad prāg dvādaśasāhasram uditaṃ daivikaṃ yugam | tad eka-saptati-guṇaṃ manvantaram iha ucyate || 79 ||
“The age of the gods said before to be twelve thousand (years), multiplied seventy-one-fold, is here named a Manvantara.”
— Manusmṛti 1.79
Manusmṛti gives only the ladder up to Brahmā's day and night. The Mahākalpa (100 years of Brahmā) is stated explicitly in the Purāṇas — e.g. Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.3.
Viṣṇu Purāṇa (1.3) 15 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā

1.3.8 — A different first step. Here fifteen nimeṣa make one kāṣṭhā; thirty kāṣṭhā, one kalā; thirty kalā, one muhūrta. The rest of the chain matches Manusmṛti.

काष्ठा पञ्चदशाख्याता निमेषा मुनिसत्तम । काष्ठात्रिंशत् कला त्रिंशत् कला मौहूर्तिको विधिः ॥
kāṣṭhā pañcadaśākhyātā nimeṣā munisattama | kāṣṭhā-triṃśat kalā triṃśat kalā mauhūrtiko vidhiḥ ||
“O best of sages, fifteen nimeṣas are called a kāṣṭhā; thirty kāṣṭhās make a kalā; thirty kalās constitute a muhūrta — such is the ordained measure.”
— Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.3.8 (Parāśara to Maitreya)

1.3.15 & 1.3.18 — Mahāyuga and Manvantara. A thousand Caturyugas are one day of Brahmā; and seventy-one Caturyugas (with an extra portion) make one Manvantara.

कृतं त्रेता द्वापरश्च कलिश्चैव चतुर्युगम् । तत्सहस्रं च ब्रह्मणो दिवसं मुने ॥
kṛtaṃ tretā dvāparaś ca kaliś caiva caturyugam | tat-sahasraṃ ca brahmaṇo divasaṃ mune ||
“Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali together are a Caturyuga; and a thousand of these, O sage, are a day of Brahmā.”
— Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.3.15
चतुर्युगाणां संख्याता साधिका ह्येकसप्ततिः । मन्वन्तरं मनोः कालः सुरादीनां च सत्तम ॥
caturyugāṇāṃ saṅkhyātā sādhikā hy eka-saptatiḥ | manvantaraṃ manoḥ kālaḥ surādīnāṃ ca sattama ||
“Seventy-one Caturyugas, with some additional years, make one Manvantara — the period of a Manu and of the gods.”
— Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.3.18
Viṣṇu Purāṇa is the text that spells out the Mahākalpa: a hundred years of Brahmā is his whole life; the half of it (a Parārdha, ≈155.52 trillion years) closed with the Pādma Mahākalpa, and the present age is the Vārāha Kalpa of the second half.
Bhāgavata Purāṇa (3.11) 3 nimeṣa = 1 kṣaṇa (atom-upward)

3.11.6–3.11.8 — Built from the atom. This scheme starts from the atom and builds downward to the day — and its ratios differ from Manusmṛti: 3 lava = 1 nimeṣa, 3 nimeṣa = 1 kṣaṇa, 5 kṣaṇa = 1 kāṣṭhā, 15 kāṣṭhā = 1 laghu, 15 laghu = 1 nāḍikā, 2 nāḍikā = 1 muhūrta.

त्रसरेणुत्रिकं भुङ्क्ते यः कालः स त्रुटिः स्मृतः । शतभागस्तु वेधः स्यात्तैस्त्रिभिस्तु लवः स्मृतः ॥ ६ ॥
trasareṇu-trikaṃ bhuṅkte yaḥ kālaḥ sa truṭiḥ smṛtaḥ | śata-bhāgas tu vedhaḥ syāt tais tribhis tu lavaḥ smṛtaḥ || 6 ||
“The time that integrates three trasareṇus is called a truṭi; a hundred truṭis make a vedha; and three vedhas make a lava.”
— Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 3.11.6
निमेषस्त्रिलवो ज्ञेय आम्नातस्ते त्रयः क्षणः । क्षणान्पञ्च विदुः काष्ठां लघु ता दश पञ्च च ॥ ७ ॥
nimeṣas tri-lavo jñeya āmnātas te trayaḥ kṣaṇaḥ | kṣaṇān pañca viduḥ kāṣṭhāṃ laghu tā daśa pañca ca || 7 ||
“Three lavas are to be known as one nimeṣa; three of these make a kṣaṇa; five kṣaṇas make a kāṣṭhā; and fifteen kāṣṭhās make a laghu.”
— Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 3.11.7
लघूनि वै समाम्नाता दश पञ्च च नाडिका । ते द्वे मुहूर्तः प्रहरः षड्यामः सप्त वा नृणाम् ॥ ८ ॥
laghūni vai samāmnātā daśa pañca ca nāḍikā | te dve muhūrtaḥ praharaḥ ṣaḍ-yāmaḥ sapta vā nṛṇām || 8 ||
“Fifteen laghus make one nāḍikā (also called daṇḍa); two daṇḍas make a muhūrta; and six or seven daṇḍas make a quarter of a day or night, by human reckoning.”
— Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 3.11.8
Because Bhāgavata builds 3 → 3 → 5 → 15 → 15, its nimeṣa comes out larger (roughly 0.53 s by that chain), showing that "nimeṣa" was not a single fixed SI value across traditions.
Sūrya Siddhānta (1.10–1.12) Astronomical / prāṇa-based

1.10–1.12 — Measurable (mūrta) time. The astronomical tradition divides time differently: it begins with the prāṇa (one breath, ≈ 4 s). Six prāṇas = 1 vināḍī (pala); sixty vināḍīs = 1 nāḍī (ghaṭikā); sixty nāḍīs = one sidereal day and night.

लोकानामन्तकृत्कालः कालोऽन्यः कलनात्मकः । स द्विधा स्थूलसूक्ष्मत्वान्मूर्तश्चामूर्त उच्यते ॥
lokānām antakṛt kālaḥ kālo 'nyaḥ kalanātmakaḥ | sa dvidhā sthūla-sūkṣmatvān mūrtaś cāmūrta ucyate ||
“Time destroys the worlds; another Time is of the nature of reckoning. It is twofold — by grossness and subtlety — called measurable (mūrta) and immeasurable (amūrta).”
— Sūrya Siddhānta 1.10
षड्भिः प्राणैर्विनाडी स्यात्तत्षष्ट्या नाडिका स्मृता । तत्त्रिंशता भवेन्मासः सावनोऽर्कोदयैस्तथा ॥
ṣaḍbhiḥ prāṇair vināḍī syāt tat-ṣaṣṭyā nāḍikā smṛtā | tat-triṃśatā bhaven māsaḥ sāvano 'rkodayais tathā ||
“Six prāṇas make a vināḍī; sixty of these a nāḍī; and sixty nāḍīs a sidereal day and night.”
— Sūrya Siddhānta 1.11–1.12
In this system the nimeṣa appears lower down: 1 truṭi = 1/33,750 s; 100 truṭi = 1 tatpara; 30 tatpara = 1 nimeṣa (≈ 0.089 s); 18 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā; 30 kāṣṭhā = 1 kalā; 30 kalā = 1 ghaṭikā (24 min); 2 ghaṭikā = 1 muhūrta.
Mahābhārata (Śānti Parva 12.231) 15 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā; muhūrta variant

12.231.12–13. “The ṛṣis, measuring time, have given names to its portions. Fifteen winks of the eye make a kāṣṭhā; thirty kāṣṭhās a kalā; thirty kalās — with a tenth of a kalā — make a muhūrta; thirty muhūrtas make one day and night.”

12.231.16–17. A month of humans is the day-and-night of the departed manes; and a year of humans is the day-and-night of the gods — the sun's northward course their day.

Here the muhūrta is slightly larger: 30 kalās + 1/10 kalā (some commentaries read “thirty kalās and three kāṣṭhās”). Otherwise the scheme follows the fifteen-nimeṣa tradition.
Nāṭyaśāstra / Kohala (Tālalakṣaṇa) 8-based musical time

The 8-fold chain. The dramatic/musical tradition measures kāla in powers of eight, and defines the kṣaṇa poetically:

1 kṣaṇa = the time for a needle to pierce a hundred lotus petals stacked together · 8 kṣaṇa = 1 lava · 8 lava = 1 kāṣṭa · 8 kāṣṭa = 1 nimeṣa · 8 nimeṣa = 1 kalā.

This is a completely different ladder from Manusmṛti's 18/30/30/30 — a reminder that "nimeṣa" was a relative unit, defined inside each system.
Arthaśāstra (2.20) 5 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā

2.20.29–38 — Kauṭilya's table.

2 tuṭa = 1 lava · 2 lava = 1 nimeṣa · 5 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā · 30 kāṣṭhā = 1 kalā · 40 kalā = 1 nālikā · 2 nālikā = 1 muhūrta · 15 muhūrta = 1 day, 15 muhūrta = 1 night.

Yet another count: five nimeṣa to a kāṣṭhā, and a different relation between kalā, nālikā and muhūrta.
Skanda Purāṇa 15 nimeṣa = 1 kāṣṭhā

Prabhāsa Khaṇḍa (measures of time). 15 nimeṣas = 1 kāṣṭhā; 30 kāṣṭhās = 1 kalā; 30 kalās = 1 muhūrta; 30 muhūrtas = day and night (24 hours); then 15 days = pakṣa; 2 pakṣas = month; 2 months = ṛtu; 3 ṛtus = ayana; 2 ayanas = a year.

अहोरात्रं मुहूर्तेन मुहूर्तं तु कलाहतम् । कलां काष्ठा हतां कृत्वा काष्ठां निमिषभाजिताम् ॥
ahorātraṃ muhūrtena muhūrtaṃ tu kalāhatam | kalāṃ kāṣṭhā hatāṃ kṛtvā kāṣṭhāṃ nimiṣa-bhājitām ||
“A day-and-night is reckoned by muhūrtas, a muhūrta divided into kalās; a kalā into kāṣṭhās, and a kāṣṭhā into nimeṣas — thus is time measured.”
— Skanda Purāṇa, Prabhāsa Khaṇḍa 19.21

Total nimeṣa in one Mahākalpa:
486,000 nimeṣa/day × 360 days/year × 311,040,000,000,000 years = ≈ 5.442 × 10²² nimeṣa (about 54.4 sextillion twinklings of the eye).
At the end of it comes Mahāpralaya — Brahmā dissolves back into Brahman, time ceases, and the cycle rests before beginning again.
A note on accuracy: a widely circulated figure states “≈ 5.6369 × 10¹⁹ nimeṣa (56.3 quintillion)” for the Mahākalpa. That value does not match the Manusmṛti chain — it works out to only ~322 billion years, i.e. about a thousand times too small. Using 486,000 nimeṣa per day and 360 days per year, the correct figure is ≈ 5.44 × 10²² nimeṣa.
